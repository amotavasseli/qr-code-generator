What would you actually pay for?

An honest page about things that do not exist yet.

Nothing on this page is built. There is no Pro plan, no checkout, and nothing to buy today. These are four ideas for people whose job the free tool almost does — and the only reason this page exists is to find out which of them, if any, are worth building.

The generator itself stays free

Worth saying plainly before anything else: the bulk generator is not going behind a paywall, and no limit is being added to it later. No cap on codes, no watermark, no signup, no expiring trial.

That is not generosity, it is the entire point of the tool. Most QR services hand you a dynamic code that quietly routes through their domain, which is why so many printed codes die when a subscription lapses. Everything here is static — your link is encoded directly into the pattern, so there is nothing anyone could switch off, including us. Charging for the codes themselves would break the one promise that makes this worth using.

So anything paid would sit around the generator, never in front of it.

The four ideas

  • Saved presets Your colours, logo, size, error correction level, filename convention and sheet layout, saved under a name and reloaded in one click. If you run the same label job every month, this is the one that stops you re-entering twelve settings every time. Stored in your own browser — still no account, still nothing uploaded.
  • Print-ready label sheets Output matched to real label stock — Avery, Herma and the common equivalents — with the exact grid, margins and bleed for a given sheet number, exported as a print-ready PDF. Feed the paper in, print, peel. No nudging a grid around in Word for an hour first.
  • An offline build A single self-contained file you download once and open with no internet connection at all. For anyone whose IT policy forbids putting internal URLs into any website, whatever that website claims. It is the same tool, just provably disconnected — and it keeps working whether or not this site does.
  • A commercial licence Not a feature: paperwork. A written commercial-use licence, a proper invoice for expenses or procurement, and a support address with a person behind it. Some organisations genuinely cannot adopt a free tool without that file to point at.

Tell us which ones matter

Ticking a box here is not a commitment to buy anything, and leaving an email does not sign you up for a mailing list. It is a vote on build order, plus a way to let you know if the thing you picked ever ships. What happens to the address is spelled out in the Privacy Policy — short version: nothing else.

The form is not wired up yet. Until it is, email contact@batchqrcodes.com with the subject "Pro interest" and say which of the four you would use, and what for. Every one gets read.

What if none of them?

That is a genuinely useful answer, and it is the most likely one. If nobody wants any of this, none of it gets built, the tool stays exactly as it is, and the time goes into more guides instead — which is probably the better outcome anyway.
